Aero Design Project

Oregon State University · Instructor: Dr. Nancy Squires

  • Focus: fundamentals of aerodynamics, stability, and control
  • Outcome: a scale model aircraft concept with a mid-mounted wing, streamlined fuselage, and tail fin optimized for directional stability
  • Skills: CAD modeling and aerodynamic analysis; balancing lift, drag, and structure; iterative team design

ME 250: Hole Puncher

In this introductory manufacturing and design course, I designed, prototyped, and built a mechanical hole puncher from scratch, using a CNC mill for precision drilling, a manual lathe for shaping metal rods, and bench tools such as a drill press and band saw. Selecting materials, holding tolerances, and troubleshooting design flaws sparked my passion for manufacturing.

ME 382: Mechanical Systems Design

Our team designed and built a remote-controlled robot within a 50 × 50 × 50 cm box and under $200 in materials to complete five tasks: sprint 5 m and back, climb three 5 cm steps, throw a ping-pong ball, kick a ball as far as possible, and retrieve a ball 5 m away. We integrated chassis, propulsion, and lifting mechanisms with motors and remote-control electronics, while managing time and budget. It was my real introduction to how mechanical, electrical, and control systems come together.

Senior Capstone: Robotic Drawer

As part of a larger Oregon State effort to build a humanoid testing lab, our team developed a robotic drawer with interchangeable handles and integrated sensors for testing how a humanoid robot opens drawers. We gathered stakeholder requirements, applied design for manufacturing and assembly, and built a working prototype that met the specifications.
